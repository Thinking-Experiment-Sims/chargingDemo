# Electrostatics Laboratory: Conduction, Induction & Grounding

## Introduction

This interactive laboratory uses the Electrostatic Charging Simulation to help you investigate how objects become electrically charged. You'll explore three key processes in electrostatics: conduction, induction, and grounding.

![Simulation Screenshot](/pictures/Screenshot%202025-04-03%20at%203.18.43%20PM.png)

## Learning Goals

By completing this activity, you will be able to:
- Distinguish between charging by conduction and charging by induction
- Explain the purpose and effect of grounding in electrostatic systems
- Predict charge distributions in conducting objects
- Relate electrostatic principles to real-world phenomena

## Pre-Lab Questions

Before using the simulation, answer these questions to activate your prior knowledge:

1. What is electric charge? What are the two types of charge?
2. What happens when objects with like charges interact? Unlike charges?
3. In conductors, which charged particles can move freely?
4. What does it mean for an object to be "grounded"?

## Part 1: Charging by Conduction

![Charging by Conduction](/pictures/Charging%20with%20conduction.png)

### Prediction
Before using the simulation, predict:
1. What will happen when a positively charged rod touches a neutral conducting ball?
2. What will happen when a negatively charged rod touches a neutral conducting ball?

### Procedure
1. Open the simulation and select "Conduction" mode
2. Set the rod to a positive charge (+5μC) using the slider
3. Move the rod to touch the ball
4. Observe what happens to:
   - The charge values
   - The ball's color
   - The movement of charges
   - The force between objects after separation
5. Reset the simulation
6. Repeat steps 2-4 but with a negatively charged rod (-5μC)

### Data Collection

| Rod Charge | Ball Charge Before Contact | Ball Charge After Contact | Direction of Electron Movement | Force After Separation |
|------------|----------------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|------------------------|
| +5μC       |                            |                           |                               |                        |
| -5μC       |                            |                           |                               |                        |

### Analysis Questions
1. In charging by conduction, how is charge transferred between objects?
2. Why do the objects repel after separation?
3. Write a general rule describing charging by conduction.
4. Why is this process sometimes called "charging by contact"?

## Part 2: Charging by Induction (Without Grounding)

![Charging by Induction](/pictures/Charging%20by%20Induction.png)

### Prediction
Before using the simulation, predict:
1. What will happen to the charge distribution in a neutral conducting ball when a charged rod is brought near (but does not touch) it?
2. Will the ball be attracted to or repelled by the rod? Explain.

### Procedure
1. Reset the simulation and select "Induction" mode
2. Set the rod to a positive charge (+5μC)
3. Move the rod near the ball WITHOUT touching it
4. Observe what happens to:
   - The charge distribution within the ball
   - The overall charge of the ball
   - The movement of charges
   - The force between the rod and ball
5. Reset and repeat with a negatively charged rod (-5μC)

### Data Collection

| Rod Charge | Charge Distribution in Ball | Net Charge on Ball | Force Between Rod and Ball |
|------------|-----------------------------|--------------------|----------------------------|
| +5μC       |                             |                    |                            |
| -5μC       |                             |                    |                            |

### Analysis Questions
1. Does the ball gain a net charge through induction without grounding? Why or why not?
2. Why are neutral conducting objects attracted to charged objects?
3. How does the charge distribution in the ball differ when using a positive versus negative rod?
4. Is the force attractive or repulsive in both cases? Explain why.

## Part 3: Charging by Induction (With Grounding)

![Grounding](/pictures/Grounding.png)

### Prediction
Before using the simulation, predict:
1. What will happen if the ball is grounded while a charged rod is nearby?
2. How will grounding affect the final charge of the ball?

### Procedure
1. Reset the simulation and select "Induction" mode
2. Set the rod to a positive charge (+5μC)
3. Move the rod near the ball WITHOUT touching it
4. While keeping the rod in position, click the "Ground" button
5. Observe the flow of charge
6. Unclick the "Ground" button (still keeping the rod in place)
7. Now move the rod away from the ball
8. Observe the final charge on the ball
9. Reset and repeat steps 2-8 with a negatively charged rod (-5μC)

### Data Collection

| Rod Charge | Ball Charge After Complete Process | Direction of Electron Flow During Grounding | Final Force Between Rod and Ball |
|------------|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| +5μC       |                                    |                                             |                                  |
| -5μC       |                                    |                                             |                                  |

### Analysis Questions
1. What is the sign of the final charge on the ball when using a positive rod? A negative rod?
2. Explain the pattern: how does the final charge on the ball relate to the charge on the rod?
3. Why does grounding result in a permanent charge on the ball, while induction without grounding doesn't?
4. Trace the flow of electrons during each step of the process.

## Part 4: Challenge Scenarios

### Challenge 1: Three-Step Process
Predict the outcome of this sequence:
1. Start with a neutral ball
2. Bring a negatively charged rod near (don't touch)
3. Ground the ball
4. Remove the ground connection (while rod is still near)
5. Remove the rod

2. The opposite charge as the rod

## Extension: Coulomb's Law Investigation

If time permits, use the simulation to explore how the electrostatic force changes with distance.

1. Place a charged rod at various distances from a charged ball
2. Record the force at each distance
3. Graph your results and determine if they match the inverse-square relationship predicted by Coulomb's Law: F ∝ 1/r²
