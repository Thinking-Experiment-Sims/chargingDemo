To fully understand the behavior of a hanging pith ball in all possible scenarios, we must carefully analyze how electrons move and how this affects the net charge and resulting electrostatic forces. Below is a detailed explanation for each combination of rod charge (positive or negative) and charging method (conduction or induction).

---

## **1. Charging by Conduction**

In conduction, the charged rod makes direct contact with the neutral pith ball, allowing electrons to flow between them. The pith ball acquires the same type of charge as the rod because of this electron transfer.

### **Scenario 1: Negatively Charged Rod**
- **Electron Flow**: Electrons flow from the negatively charged rod to the neutral pith ball.
- **Net Charge of Pith Ball**: The pith ball gains excess electrons and becomes negatively charged.
- **Electrostatic Force**: After contact, both the rod and the pith ball are negatively charged. Since like charges repel, the pith ball is repelled by the rod and moves away.

### **Scenario 2: Positively Charged Rod**
- **Electron Flow**: Electrons flow from the neutral pith ball to the positively charged rod.
- **Net Charge of Pith Ball**: The pith ball loses electrons and becomes positively charged (electron deficient).
- **Electrostatic Force**: After contact, both the rod and the pith ball are positively charged. Since like charges repel, the pith ball is repelled by the rod and moves away.

---

## **2. Charging by Induction**

In induction, the charged rod is brought near (but does not touch) the neutral pith ball. This causes a redistribution of charges within the pith ball due to electrostatic forces. If grounding is introduced, electrons can flow between the pith ball and ground, leaving it permanently charged.

### **Scenario 3: Negatively Charged Rod (No Grounding)**
- **Electron Redistribution**: The negative charges (electrons) in the pith ball are repelled by the negatively charged rod. Electrons move to the far side of the pith ball, leaving the near side positively charged.
- **Net Charge of Pith Ball**: The pith ball remains neutral overall but becomes polarized (positive near side, negative far side).
- **Electrostatic Force**: The positive near side is attracted to the negatively charged rod, so the pith ball moves toward the rod.

### **Scenario 4: Negatively Charged Rod (With Grounding)**
- **Electron Redistribution**: As in Scenario 3, electrons are repelled to the far side of the pith ball.
- **Electron Flow to Ground**: If grounded, these repelled electrons flow out of the pith ball into the ground.
- **Net Charge of Pith Ball**: The pith ball becomes positively charged because it has lost some electrons.
- **Electrostatic Force**: After grounding, the positively charged pith ball is repelled by the negatively charged rod.

### **Scenario 5: Positively Charged Rod (No Grounding)**
- **Electron Redistribution**: The positive charge on the rod attracts electrons in the neutral pith ball. Electrons move to the near side of the pith ball, leaving its far side positively charged.
- **Net Charge of Pith Ball**: The pith ball remains neutral overall but becomes polarized (negative near side, positive far side).
- **Electrostatic Force**: The negative near side is attracted to the positively charged rod, so the pith ball moves toward the rod.

### **Scenario 6: Positively Charged Rod (With Grounding)**
- **Electron Redistribution**: As in Scenario 5, electrons are attracted to the near side of the pith ball.
- **Electron Flow from Ground**: If grounded, additional electrons flow into the pith ball from ground to neutralize its positive regions.
- **Net Charge of Pith Ball**: The pith ball becomes negatively charged because it has gained extra electrons.
- **Electrostatic Force**: After grounding, the negatively charged pith ball is repelled by the positively charged rod.

## Key Takeaways
1. In conduction:
   - Direct contact results in permanent charging with a net charge *similar* to that of the rod.
   - Like charges repel after contact.

2. In induction:
   - Without grounding, only temporary polarization occurs, leading to attraction between opposite charges on nearby surfaces.
   - With grounding, permanent charging occurs with a net charge *opposite* to that of the inducing rod.

3. Electron movement explains all behaviors:
   - Electrons move *from* areas of high electron density (negative charge) *to* areas with low electron density (positive charge or ground).
